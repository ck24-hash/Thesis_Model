Greenhouse demand

Existing PV

The output of the PV already on site is the workbook's existing PV column (step 2). It serves the same load as Voltiris and is not sized.

Voltiris

Grid

Automatic limits: import 2 × the year's highest load, export 1.1 × the year's highest PV.

Regime

CH: automatic picks A or B from the hours of use (DUP) without battery, and re-checks each size whose DUP crosses the threshold. FR: the TURPE option (LU or CU) the case chooses. NL: one regime. Tariff values are changed with the JSON overrides below.

Battery

The battery model always applies params/ancillary/<country>.json: on is its normal behaviour (25% of the window and power kept out of the dispatch, paid per reserved kW and year). Off is done with overrides in the case file: {"ancillary": {"rate_per_kw_year": 0, "reserve": {"mode": "percent", "frac": 0}}}.

Sizing

How the proposed size is chosen
  1. Baseline. The year without battery is optimised once. Its bill (energy, capacity charges, export revenue) is what every size is compared with.
  2. Each size in the list. The whole year is optimised again with that battery, quarter hour by quarter hour, knowing the year in advance. For that size the model computes:
    • bill saving = bill without battery − bill with battery
    • yearly value = bill saving + ancillary revenue (0 when switched off) − EMS costs (fixed cost and fees)
    • investment = battery price after subsidy + EMS hardware
    • payback = investment ÷ yearly value (never when the value is zero or negative)
    • lifetime profit = yearly value × battery life − investment
  3. Swiss regime check. When a size's hours of use (DUP) land in the other regime, that size is solved twice more, with the DUP held in the starting regime and under the other regime, and the better of the two is kept.
  4. Choice. The objective below picks one size among those tested.
  5. Fine grid (optional). Extra sizes near the chosen one are solved, then the choice is made again over all sizes tested.
  6. Detail. The chosen size's dispatch gives the bills, monthly tables and payback scenarios.

The model only compares the sizes it solves: it does not search between them. A finer list gives a more precise size and takes longer.

Makes a list from a range and an interval, e.g. 100 to 1,500 every 100 gives 15 sizes.

Check the setup to see the exact list the model will solve.
