Greenhouse demand

The site's electricity load, heat demand and CO2 demand, in one input file. Its time steps are the run's. Heat demand is used when the system has a heat source (the gas boiler, the heat pump), CO2 demand when it has a CO2 source (the gas boiler, the external CO2).

CO2 demand i

Voltiris

The modules' output is the yield of one module times the module count. It must run, and is added to the network together with the existing PV, behind the site's one meter, as in the reference model. Its price, subsidy and business model are on the general pages.

Modules

Existing PV

The measured output of the PV already on site. It must run, is not sized, and is added to the network together with Voltiris, as in the reference model.

Grid

The connection and the regime. The tariff comes from the market (general page Market); its day-ahead price, where the tariff uses one (NL, FR), is this block's input file.

Connection

Default: automatic (empty).

Regime i

CH: A or B from the hours of use (DUP) without battery, re-checked for each battery size. FR: the TURPE option (LU or CU). NL: one regime.

Battery

Its technology, the part kept for grid services, and how its size is chosen. Its price, subsidy and EMS are on the general pages.

Check against the reference sweep i

Fine grid, and how the sweep chooses
  1. Baseline. The year without battery is optimised once; every size is compared with its bill.
  2. Each size. The whole year is optimised with that battery: bill saving, yearly value (bill saving + ancillary revenue − EMS costs), investment (battery after subsidy + EMS hardware), payback, lifetime profit.
  3. Swiss regime check. A size whose hours of use (DUP) land in the other regime is solved twice more; the better is kept.
  4. Choice. The objective picks one size among those tested; the fine grid then refines around it.

External CO2

Liquid CO2 bought and delivered to the greenhouse, at the price of its input file (EUR per kg, any step: a daily or monthly curve over the year is fine) or at a fixed price. At every quarter hour the optimisation buys CO2 or burns gas in the boiler for its flue gas CO2, whichever costs less; with it the CO2 demand is always met.

Heat pump

Electricity in, heat out, sized in kW of heat. Without a data center its heat source is the outside air. At each step the model takes heat from whichever source is cheaper: the heat pump's electricity (at the step's price, or PV) or the boiler's gas. Its price, O&M and life are on the Commercial page.

The heat pump gives no CO2: without the gas boiler or the external CO2, the greenhouse's CO2 demand is not part of this run.

Market

Where the site is: the country, region and utility select the grid tariff, and the country and region select the subsidies and the ancillary rate. The market's currency is the run's.

Tariff from the setup check

Commercial

Who owns the modules, what the parts cost, how long they last, and what the battery's reserve earns. The grid tariff is on the Market page, the subsidies and EMS on their own pages.

Business model

EaaS: Voltiris owns the modules and the grower pays per MWh of Voltiris energy used on site; the system then changes the grid's export value and bounds.

Prices, lifetimes, revenue

The values come from the parameter files; any change holds for this run. ↺ Defaults puts a group back to the file values.

In numbers from the setup check

Subsidies

Subsidies on the Voltiris modules and on the battery, per country and region: a percent, a fixed amount, or a percent up to a threshold size (CH's OCAN rule: yearly load / 365 × factor).

Values

Off: no subsidy for that technology in this run (its share and amount set to 0, written into the case file's overrides). The gas boiler, heat pump and heat storage have no subsidy in the model.

In numbers from the setup check

EMS

The energy management system that runs the battery: a fixed yearly cost, hardware bought once with the battery (not subsidised, not replaced), and fees on the ancillary revenue and on the bill saving.

Costs and fees

    Technical: what was chosen and what it does
    
    
    
      
    Each piece of equipment at its size
    
      
    
      
    How a size is chosen: the optimisation adds capacity while one more unit saves more per year than it costs per year ("One more unit costs": its investment spread over its life + fixed O&M; the battery's over the PV life with replacements, less its reserve revenue). The last unit is used only in the hours the equipment runs at full size, so few hours at full size mean a size set by the peak; when a block must meet a demand on its own, its size is that demand's peak. Use: full-load hours (energy over the year ÷ size), or full cycles for storage.
